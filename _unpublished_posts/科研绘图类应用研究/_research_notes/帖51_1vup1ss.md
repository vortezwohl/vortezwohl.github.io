- 正文: I am trying to finish this manuscript in the cancer-virology field and I need to make a graphical abstract. Since the rampant use of biorender, I feel like the scientific illustration became boring and template-based (although I used it for schematics, etc). Does anyone ever used any apps or resource or software to make scientific illustration (preferably open source)? I can play around with illustrator if I need to. No background in design whatsoever but it will be a nice change to get anything other than something from BioRender. Any suggestions appreciated. Thanks in advance

[2026-08-21|blacksite|7] I like using a combination of Adobe Illustrator and [Servier Medical Art (SMART)](https://smart.servier.com/) database. You can download the graphics from SMART and load them into Illustrator, release clipping masks, and then customize everything as much as you want! Depends on your field if the SMART database will be good enough, but that’s my go-to!
[2026-08-21|BananaBird1|3] Illustrator or Inkscape are the best. The basics just take a few days to learn. If you are in a time-crunch and can’t learn more advanced techniques, you can use free vector asset libraries like this: [https://bioart.niaid.nih.gov](https://bioart.niaid.nih.gov)
[2026-08-21|Oligonucleotide123|2] This is not a journal cover or an art piece, it's meant to convey the main story to your paper. Use whatever does that most clearly, even if it's biorender. Nobody cares if your mouse stock image is the same as someone else's.
[2026-08-22|Rubejrs|2] I would say unless you're proficient at illustrator to the point where you can adequately express your vision, stick to biorender. I am with you, I think it's a noble cause to not want to use the same stock figures and I believe science can only benefit from a more personal touch. However, if you don't have the ability or training to visually capture ideas with enough universal appeal then a handmade figure will fall flat and look bad.
