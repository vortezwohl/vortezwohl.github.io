- 正文: Lucidchart is worth paying for when you're responsible for detailed process diagrams that other people need to keep editing. For an occasional flowchart, I'd start with draw.io. This is a researched comparison, not a hands-on review. Think of an operations lead mapping an approval process across departments. The diagram needs consistent symbols and a readable layout. Colleagues need to change it without learning a text-based diagram language. Lucidchart's premium templates and shape libraries may help, especially when the same kinds of diagrams recur. Free already includes sharing and comments. The pricing page advertises three editable documents and 75 shapes per document, though Lucid's help materials still say 60. Check what your account permits. Individual removes the document and shape limits and adds premium templates and libraries. It doesn't include revision history; Team does. That matters when several people maintain the process and you need to recover an earlier version. Lucidchart pricing is $9/month for Individual or $10/user/month for Team when billed annually, excluding tax. That's $108 a year or $120 per user a year, respectively. Check the total and billing period at checkout if you want to pay monthly. The seven-day paid-feature trial requires payment details and bills automatically unless canceled. ### Where the free options fit draw.io, also known as diagrams.net, is a free visual editor. You can keep diagrams on your device or in your chosen cloud storage instead of Lucid's account. Its free desktop app runs on Windows, macOS and Linux and can create diagrams offline. If you mainly need boxes, connectors and a file you control, that's a strong reason to skip another subscription. You do need to decide where the file lives and who handles backups. Mermaid suits diagrams maintained alongside software documentation. You write a text description that becomes a diagram, and GitHub can render it in Markdown files and pull requests. Keeping that source beside the code lets the team review diagram changes with code changes. It's a less attractive default when the process owner wants to drag a shape rather than edit syntax. That's a workflow choice, not a claim that paid diagrams are more professional. ### What happens when you stop paying Lucidchart works through a browser, but offline editing needs preparation. Open the document before disconnecting and leave both the document and browser open until changes sync after reconnecting. You can't open another document or create a new one while offline. For routine disconnected work, draw.io desktop is the simpler option. Canceling a paid Lucid plan stops renewal and returns it to Free at the end of the paid period. Documents remain viewable, but full editing is no longer available. Lucid states a no-refund cancellation policy. Export before you leave. PDF, PNG and SVG give you copies of the drawing, but Lucid says comments and notes don't appear in those image exports. Paid plans also support Visio export. If another editor needs to take over later, open a representative exported diagram there before committing a whole process library. I'd choose Individual for a solo professional who repeatedly needs larger diagrams and premium templates. Team needs a specific team feature, such as revision history, to earn the extra cost. Stay with draw.io for ordinary visual diagrams, or Mermaid for developer-owned documentation. Before paying, use an actual approval flow and ask the person who will maintain it to change a step. If the free option handles that comfortably, keep the money.
