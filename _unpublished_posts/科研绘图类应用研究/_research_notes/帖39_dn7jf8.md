# 帖39 [Q] What exactly makes a graph "publication quality?"

- 正文: First of all, I realize this question is more about data visualization than statistics, so I apologize if it breaks any of the rules. Many professional graphing and data visualization software will often advertise that they produce "publication quality" graphs, "publication ready" graphs, or a variation of that phrase. My question is, what exactly does that mean? Obviously, in this context the word "publication quality" is pretty much synonymous with "high quality," or more specifically a quality that is good enough to appear in a peer-reviewed journal (or some other equally prestigious publication). Almost every scientist or professional (including myself) who reads the phrase "publication quality" has an intuitive understanding of what that means based on their previous experience with other high quality data visualization. But I would like to move beyond the intuitive understanding and explicitly list or categorize the specific elements that make a graph "publication quality." Based on some preliminary thought, there are two overarching standards by which to judge a graph: 1) the analytic quality of the data, and 2) the visual aesthetics of the graph. The first one is certainly the most important one since it involves the subject of the graph, i.e. how well does your graph get your point across. Point 2, on the other hand, is purely about how good your graph looks, although that can affect point 1, since aesthetics do also help get a point across. And there's nothing wrong with wanting your graphs to look "fancy," as long as that doesn't interfere with the clarity of the data. Figuring out what satisfies the first point is arguably more difficult and more elusive since it highly depends on the kind of data you're analyzing. Different data have different requirements. The second point, on the other hand, is perhaps more universal, although in certain ways more challenging since it requires a degree of artistic skills that a lot of scientists don't really have. I for one have a harder time satisfying the second requirement than I have the first. That said, what are your thoughts on this topic? What does, in your opinion, constitute a "publication quality" graph, both in terms of data analysis and visual aesthetics? Also, if you have any good sources on the subject, feel free to recommend them. I know Edward Tufte has written about this topic, but I haven't gotten around to reading his books yet.

  [2019-10-26|jmhimara|1] I agree that when software promise "publication quality" they're primarily referring to the visual aesthetics of the plot. The data is entirely dependent on the user. However, I would argue that it's a lot more than just resolution. Color schemes, lines and shapes, proportions, typeface, etc., also matter. Even something like Excel can produce high-resolution graphs, but most of them are pretty ugly nevertheless. I would not consider Excel graphs "publication quality." I understand that most journals may care only about resolution, but I was trying to get beyond that. I think the phrase "publication quality" implies more than just resolution.
